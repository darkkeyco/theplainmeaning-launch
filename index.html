Hi, I'm Tara.

I taught middle school English before I went to law school. It was the best legal training I ever got. If you can explain a semicolon to thirteen-year-olds right after lunch, you can explain an operating agreement to anybody.

I've been a licensed attorney in South Carolina since 2014, and I've spent most of my career on the public side of the law. I was a staff attorney at the South Carolina Supreme Court and the Administrative Law Court, and I've been counsel to state licensing boards and a state regulator. I've also written and rewritten hundreds of model policies for school districts, and then trained the people who had to follow them. These days I work in healthcare regulation.

Every one of those jobs had the same setup. Somebody writes a rule, and then a person who never saw the draft has to run her business around it. I've spent my career in the gap between those two people, and the ones who get hurt there rarely have a lawyer on retainer. It's the founder who signed the first contract someone emailed her. It's the two friends who formed an LLC and never wrote down who owns what. It's the business owner who figured the logo she paid for was hers. Most of the legal trouble I've seen started with a document nobody read in plain English.

If you saw yourself in one of those three, The Plain Meaning is for you. You'll know what to look for before you sign, and you'll know when it's time to call a lawyer. I teach the legal basics of starting and running a small business in plain English, from picking an entity to reading contracts and protecting what you create. Reading the contract costs you an evening, and skipping it can cost you the business.

The Plain Meaning is education, so I don't give legal advice or review your documents here. Your situation deserves a lawyer who knows your facts and your state. Part of my job is making sure you walk into that conversation ready, with your questions written down and your documents in order.

I also teach workshops for chambers of commerce and the groups that support founders. And I teach yoga, which turns out to be the same skill, helping people stay calm while they do something uncomfortable.
